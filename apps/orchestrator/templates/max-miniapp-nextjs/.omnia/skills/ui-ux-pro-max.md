# UI/UX Pro Max — creative virtuoso lens

This capability combines product design, art direction, interaction design and
mobile craft. It does not provide a layout recipe. Its job is to raise the
quality of the decisions you make for this exact product.

The executor appends a compact project-matched slice of Omnia's vendored
`ui-ux-pro-max` knowledge base below: colour, typography, visual-language,
compatible-icon, chart and high-severity UX evidence. Recommendations requiring
a dependency absent from the MAX starter are discarded by the executor.

## Mastery protocol

1. **Find the emotional promise.** Name what the user should feel during the
   primary loop: in control, energised, reassured, curious, rewarded, focused or
   something more specific. Translate it into hierarchy, density, rhythm and
   feedback — not decorative copy.
2. **Design from the task object.** Let the product's meaningful object shape the
   interface: workout, booking, order, lesson, balance, collection, route or
   conversation. Do not start from cards, dashboards or a hero section.
3. **Explore three real directions.** Change composition, type voice, geometry,
   surface behaviour, information density and motion grammar. Colour-only
   variants are one direction pretending to be three.
4. **Choose one signature.** Give the product one memorable, useful expression:
   an animated training ring, tactile booking timeline, living progress field,
   spatial collection, contextual coach response or another domain-native
   moment. It must strengthen the primary action.
5. **Build a delight map.** Give the primary loop clear anticipation, immediate
   acknowledgement, visible progress, a meaningful completion response and an
   easy next move. Add haptics only where the MAX bridge supports them.
6. **Create a visual system, not a pile of styles.** Establish semantic colour
   roles, a deliberate type scale, spacing rhythm, radii logic, icon weight and
   three motion tiers. Allow contrast and asymmetry where they create hierarchy.
7. **Critique the rendered product.** Ask: what feels generic, what competes with
   the primary action, what has no state, what jumps during loading, what lacks
   touch feedback, and what would a strong designer remove? Apply the critique
   before `done`.

## Craft bar

- The first viewport is already the product and makes the next action obvious.
- Content sounds written for this business and scenario, never like UI filler.
- Empty, loading, error, success and populated states share the same composition.
- Controls have at least 44px touch targets; pressed, focus and disabled states
  are intentional. Dynamic numbers use tabular figures.
- Nested radii are optically coherent. Borders separate; layered shadows express
  elevation. Avoid card-inside-card stacks.
- Headings balance cleanly and body copy wraps naturally at 360px. The interface
  never depends on hover.
- Motion is interruptible and causal: transform/opacity first, no catch-all
  transitions, no perpetual decoration, and a useful reduced-motion equivalent.
- One excellent transition serving the primary loop is more valuable than ten
  generic fade-ins.

## Freedom with standards

The project-matched candidates below are evidence, not a selected preset.
Compare them inside the three-direction exploration, combine or transform them,
and reject anything that conflicts with the product. Borrow principles, never a
recognisable composition. Never copy a landing-page structure into a MAX Mini
App and never make two unrelated products share the same visual skeleton.
